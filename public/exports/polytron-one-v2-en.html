System Overview

Overview

POLYTRON ONE brings camera management, live monitoring, AI event detection, alarm response, and recording review into one platform. Operators can move from an alert to verification and response; investigators can retrieve footage and retain evidence; administrators can maintain devices, accounts, and access. This guide follows those working tasks, from signing in to monitoring, investigation, and system maintenance.

Platform Capabilities

  • Centralized Video Management: Manage camera details, connection state, location, protocol, stream URL, recording schedule, and alarm configuration in one place. Group devices by building, floor, or operational area to support monitoring, search, Playback, and resource permissions.
  • Live Monitoring and Multiple Views: Organize live video in single-camera views, multi-camera grids, tabs, multiple views, or the real-time 3D view. Switch camera groups for the task and use spatial or alarm context to locate the scene you need to inspect.
  • AI Video Analysis: Analyze camera footage for events such as crowding, line crossing, loitering, falls, unusual behavior, and unattended objects. Other documented capabilities include people counting, face recognition, mask detection, and heat maps. Select the capabilities available in the deployment and verify relevant results against the footage.
  • Detection Areas and Rules: Draw detection areas, crossing lines, or restricted zones, then configure direction, trigger conditions, duration, people thresholds, schedules, and algorithms. For example, detect entry outside working hours or trigger an alarm when dwell time or crowd size meets the configured condition.
  • Alarm Response: When a detection rule triggers, the platform connects the alarm to live video, notifications, event details, and surrounding footage. Operators can confirm, annotate, close, or mark an alarm as false, then follow the relevant work-order or internal response process and retain the handling record.
  • Recording Review and Evidence: Find recordings by camera, date, type, and time range. Use Playback controls and speed adjustments to inspect the sequence of events. Alarm-linked clips provide context; export only the relevant recording range when evidence is required.
  • Notifications and Event Tracking: Review severity, time, location, camera, state, and handling progress in Notification Center. Filter by keyword, time, state, or type, then open details to inspect footage, AI annotations, and notes throughout the event lifecycle.
  • Accounts, Permissions, and Configuration: Assign roles, feature permissions, and camera-resource scope by responsibility. Maintain display, storage, log-retention, recording, alarm, and system settings. Separate administration, response, and viewing duties, and review whether access still matches each user’s work.
  • System Integration: Connect video resources through protocols such as RTSP and ONVIF, and integrate with VMS, 3D visualization, alarm, and business systems as configured for the project. Integrations can associate video, events, device state, and detection results; use the project configuration to determine the actual input and output scope.

Login Authentication

Login

Sign in to POLYTRON ONE with the account assigned by your supplier or administrator. After authentication, the platform displays the pages, cameras, and actions allowed by the account’s role.

Overview

You do not need to register an account yourself. Obtain your assigned username and initial password before first use. Access to Live View, Playback, Cameras, Notification Center, and settings depends on the account’s permissions.

First Sign-In

  1. Obtain an account from the supplier, project administrator, or system owner.
  2. Enter the username and initial password on the login page.
  3. Change the initial password if the system prompts you to do so.
  4. Confirm access to the modules and cameras needed for your work. Contact an administrator if the scope is incorrect.

Signing In

Enter your username and password on the login page. After sign-in, the platform loads the appropriate menus, cameras, notifications, and actions.

Permissions also control individual operations. For example, users without camera-management permission cannot add or edit cameras. Users with view-only access cannot change alarm states or submit handling notes. Account, role, log, and system-configuration access is controlled separately.

Accounts and Permissions

  1. An authorized administrator reviews or configures the required feature permissions and camera scope in User Role Settings.
  2. The administrator creates the account in Account Settings, enters the email address, initial password, and other required information, and assigns the appropriate role.
  3. After saving, the user signs in to verify the required pages, cameras, and actions.

Remember Me

On a trusted device, Remember Me can fill in the username and password the next time you open the login page. Use it only on a controlled personal computer or workstation.

Do not save credentials on public computers, temporary troubleshooting devices, or shared terminals. Sign out when you finish.

Password Recovery

Select Forgot Password, verify through the email address bound to the account, and set a new password. Use the new password to sign in. Resetting the password does not change the account’s role, feature permissions, or camera-resource scope.

Usage Guidelines

  • Sign-in failures: Check the account identifier, username or email address, password, and whether the account and role are enabled. If sign-in succeeds but resources are missing, review both the role and camera-resource permissions.
  • Shared workstations: Sign out before leaving, ending a shift, or changing accounts so another person cannot continue using the session.
  • Account changes: Avoid retaining initial or weak passwords. Change credentials if they may have been exposed, and update roles and camera scope when responsibilities or project access change.
  • Reporting issues: Provide the account identifier, occurrence time, error message, and a screenshot to the administrator or support team. Do not send the password.

Forgot Password

Recover access by verifying through the email address bound to your account and setting a new password. A password reset changes the sign-in credential, not the account’s role or camera permissions.

Overview

Select Forgot Password on the login page and enter the email address bound to the account. After validation, the platform sends a verification code or reset link. Complete verification, set a password that meets the current rules, and sign in again.

Reset Your Password

  1. Select Forgot Password on the login page.
  2. Enter the email address bound to the account.
  3. Open the verification email and use the code or reset link before it expires.
  4. Enter and confirm a new password. Follow the length and character requirements shown for the deployment.
  5. Return to the login page after the reset and sign in with the new password. Confirm that your existing modules and camera resources remain available.

Verification and Password Rules

The email address must be bound to a valid account. Verification codes and reset links expire; start recovery again if the verification has expired.

The old password cannot be used for a new sign-in after the reset. Whether other devices must authenticate again depends on the deployed session and security policy. Role, feature, and camera-resource permissions are unchanged.

Troubleshooting and Precautions

  • No email: Check the address, account binding, spam folder, and mail-filtering rules. If the message is still missing, ask an administrator to verify the account email, mail service, and sending configuration.
  • Invalid verification: Use the valid code or link from the current recovery attempt. Request a new one if it has expired.
  • Password choice: Follow the platform rules and avoid predictable information such as sequential digits, birthdays, phone numbers, or the account name.
  • Access still fails: Report the account email, occurrence time, and error message to an administrator. Do not include passwords or valid verification codes.

System Information

System Information shows CPU, memory, and disk usage. Administrators and operations staff can use these indicators to investigate video stutter, recording-write failures, slow Playback, and delayed page responses.

Overview

Live video, AI analysis, recording writes, Playback, and notifications compete for system resources. Look for sustained trends and associated symptoms rather than judging the system from a single reading.

Resource Indicators

  • CPU Usage: Indicates current processor load. For sustained high usage, examine video decoding, AI analysis, concurrent access, recording work, and background services.
  • Memory Usage (RAM): Shows memory and cache pressure. Check service behavior and usage over time for persistent growth, unusual allocation, or unreleased cache.
  • Disk Usage: Shows storage consumed by recordings, logs, exports, and cache. Insufficient capacity can affect recording writes, exports, and logging; review retention and capacity before space is exhausted.
  • Operational Assessment: Assess resource pressure alongside camera counts, AI tasks, recording schedules, and system logs. Resource indicators support routine checks and investigation; they do not replace fault-specific diagnosis.

When to Use It

  • Check resources during routine inspections. For live-video stutter, examine CPU and memory; for slow Playback, consider storage-read pressure and memory use.
  • Check available storage when recordings are missing or exports fail. Observe resource changes after adding cameras, AI rules, or recording work.
  • Use trends and relevant logs to help operations assess configuration changes, server upgrades, or additional storage when pressure persists.

Usage Guidelines

Correlate unusual readings with camera availability, AI tasks, recording schedules, alarm records, and system logs. Do not delete footage or interrupt operations solely because resource use is high.

For sustained CPU or memory pressure, reduce unnecessary concurrent viewing and review task configuration. For growing disk use, examine recording and log retention, exports, and cache, then clean up or expand storage in accordance with project requirements.

Alarm Overview

Alarm Overview summarizes total, pending, and resolved alarms. Use it to assess outstanding work and open the notification list to investigate events that need attention.

Overview

Alarm counts help reveal trends and handling progress. To assess an individual risk, review severity, location, type, and footage rather than relying on counts alone.

Indicators and Interpretation

  • Total Alarms: Review the number of recorded alarms in the current reporting scope, including handled and unhandled events, and watch for unusual growth.
  • Pending Alarms: Review events that still need classification, confirmation, or resolution. This count indicates outstanding work, not the severity of each event.
  • Resolved Alarms: Review events that have completed the handling process and compare them with pending work to assess progress.
  • Status and Prioritization: Changing counts can indicate an event surge, response workload, or a need to review classification or detection rules. Prioritize high-severity alarms, personnel safety, critical areas, and recurring event types.
  • Details and Response Review: Use the notification list to inspect type, time, location, camera, state, and related video. For a persistent backlog, review response procedures, staffing, and false-positive patterns.

Review and Handle Alarms

  1. Open notifications and locate events by time, state, type, camera, or location.
  2. Open an alarm’s details and review the event, AI annotations, handling notes, and footage before and after the trigger.
  3. Confirm, classify, annotate, close, or mark the event as a False Alarm according to the verification result. Check that the list and statistics update.
  4. After reviewing the batch, clear the relevant reminder state through the available read or handling action. Clearing a reminder does not replace any response that is still required.

Usage Guidelines

During a shift, watch for a growing backlog and prioritize high-risk or ongoing incidents. For recurring alarms, review camera rules, detection areas, algorithm configuration, and scene interference.

Use Alarm Overview to identify exceptions and prioritize work. Complete verification and response in the notification or alarm details.

Camera Information

Camera Information summarizes total, online, and offline devices for routine checks, onboarding verification, and fault investigation.

Overview

Offline devices may be unable to provide live video, record footage, or run detection. Use count changes to establish the affected scope, then inspect individual cameras. A device’s current state alone does not prove that recordings exist for an earlier period.

Camera Indicators

  • Total Cameras: Compare the number of cameras added to the platform with the site inventory to identify missing, duplicate, or retired devices.
  • Online Cameras: Check how many devices are shown as online. Treat connectivity as a starting point and verify the stream, recording, and analysis capabilities needed for the task.
  • Offline Cameras: Identify devices with interrupted connectivity or unavailable video. Possible causes include power or network issues, invalid stream addresses, credentials or permissions, and service-connection failures.
  • Operational Impact and Investigation: Camera faults can affect live viewing, recording, AI detection, and alarms. Use Camera Management to investigate the device name, IP address, location, protocol, stream URL, group, and permissions.

When to Use It

  • Check camera availability at the start of a shift. For missing live video, recordings, or expected alarms, first identify the state of the affected camera.
  • After deployment, bulk onboarding, or device changes, verify the platform against the site inventory. For multiple offline devices, investigate shared network or service causes alongside system logs.

Troubleshooting

  1. Open Camera Management and check IP address, power, network, protocol, stream URL, and credentials. If multiple devices fail together, investigate switches, VMS services, and server connections.
  2. Reconcile count differences against missing, duplicate, retired, or incorrectly grouped devices. Use discovery or manual maintenance as needed.
  3. If an online device still has no image or recording, check stream configuration, codec parameters, protocol compatibility, and browser Playback capability. Do not rely on the online indicator alone.

Camera Selection

Find cameras in Live View and assign them to panes for temporary inspection, routine monitoring, or side-by-side comparison.

Overview

Choose the monitoring task and layout before selecting cameras. Use a dedicated tab for recurring work and a temporary selection for an incident that requires focused attention.

Camera Selection Functions

  • Select the Destination Pane: Click the destination pane and confirm its blue border or highlight. The next camera you add loads in that pane.
  • Search and Browse Cameras: Search by camera identifier, name, IP address, or installation location, or browse the building, floor, area, or operational group.
  • Add and Remove Cameras: After adding a camera, confirm the expected feed and its name or state. Removing a camera returns the pane to its empty state so you can add another device.
  • Clear All Cameras: Remove the cameras from the current Live View layout when you need to rebuild a temporary or task-specific view.
  • Automatically Fill Panes: Fill empty panes with available cameras according to the current layout capacity and camera-list order.
  • State and Saved Views: Check device and access indicators, including offline, abnormal, or unauthorized states. The camera-to-pane arrangement can be temporary or saved as a view or tab according to system configuration.

Recommended Workflow

  1. Create or open the relevant tab and choose a grid layout.
  2. Select the destination pane, then find a camera through search or the hierarchy.
  3. Add the camera and verify its source, name, and Playback state. Remove, replace, or automatically fill cameras as needed.
  4. Clear the selection to rebuild the layout. If a feed does not appear, check device connectivity, onboarding and stream URL, network access, and account permissions.

View Operations

View Operations provides snapshots, stream switching, analysis, fullscreen, recording indicators, multiple views, and camera details while you inspect live video.

Overview

Available actions depend on camera type, connection protocol, video state, and account permissions. For example, 360° multiple views require a compatible camera, while PTZ and 3D context require their respective capabilities and configuration.

Available Actions

  • Real-Time 3D: Open the roaming or 3D scene view to relate the camera feed to buildings, floors, areas, and device locations.
  • Snapshot: Capture the current main video image and save or download it locally to retain a scene for later review.
  • Switch to Substream: A Substream generally uses less bandwidth at lower image quality. Use it for constrained networks, multiple simultaneous feeds, or resource pressure.
  • Video Analysis: Inspect detection boxes, target recognition, people counts, and analysis such as line crossing, loitering, or falls to understand a detection or alarm.
  • Fullscreen: Enlarge the current video for scene inspection, event verification, or review.
  • Recording State: Use the recording indicator to check whether recording is active. To verify historical coverage, open Playback and inspect the required period.
  • Multiple Views: POLYTRON 360° cameras can show multiple directional views. Select a subview to make that direction the main view and inspect another part of the same space.
  • Camera Details and State: Review the camera’s name, location, group, state, and associated position, and maintain relevant details in Camera Management. Distinguish playing, loading, stream error, offline, unauthorized, and recording indicators before troubleshooting.

Usage Guidelines

  1. For an incident, first inspect the scene in a single pane or fullscreen, then capture a snapshot, review analysis annotations, or open the associated 3D location as needed.
  2. If multiple feeds stutter or bandwidth is limited, switch to a Substream and check whether Playback improves. Reassess stream quality when detail is required.
  3. For 360° cameras, inspect the relevant directions through multiple views. If a control is unavailable, check camera capabilities, protocol, recording configuration, and permissions before treating it as a fault.

PTZ Control

Use PTZ Control to adjust a compatible camera’s direction, speed, zoom, focus, and iris remotely. It supports following moving targets, inspecting distant detail, and verifying alarm scenes.

Overview

Before using PTZ, confirm that the camera supports it, the control protocol is configured, and the account has permission. Controls may be unavailable if these conditions are not met.

Set a suitable direction and movement speed before zooming into the target. Adjust focus or iris if needed, and verify the result in the camera feed.

PTZ Functions

  • Directional Movement: Move up, down, left, or right to change the viewing area. The center control may start automatic movement, stop movement, or restore a default state, depending on the camera and configuration.
  • Movement Speed: Choose a slow, medium, or fast speed for precise alignment, broad repositioning, or following a moving target. The setting can also affect some other view-adjustment operations.
  • Zoom: Zoom in to inspect distant people, equipment, plates, or an alarm area. Zoom out to restore a wider view.
  • Focus: Adjust near or far focus if the image becomes blurred after zooming. Automatic and manual focus availability depends on the camera.
  • Iris: Adjust the light entering the lens for night, backlit, bright, or low-light scenes. Available control depends on the model, protocol, and relevant configuration.
  • Inspection and Patrol: Combine direction, speed, zoom, and focus to inspect an alarm scene, follow a target, or check distant detail. These controls also support remote inspection of halls, roads, parking areas, and warehouses.

When to Use It

  • Inspect an alarm area and its surroundings, or manually follow people and vehicles using direction and speed controls.
  • Zoom into entrances, stairs, parking spaces, equipment rooms, or distant corridors, and adjust focus or iris for difficult lighting.
  • When immediate attendance is not possible, use the feed to help decide whether an on-site response is needed. Remote inspection supports verification but does not replace the response procedure.

Tab Management

Use tabs to retain different camera combinations and grid layouts and switch between routine monitoring, inspections, and temporary incidents.

Overview

For many cameras or tasks, split views by area, floor, responsibility, or incident instead of placing every feed on one page. Keep recurring views consistently named.

Tab Functions

  • Create and Rename Tabs: Create a tab for an area or task, choose its layout, and add cameras. Use short names such as Lobby, Parking, or Night Patrol to make views easy to identify.
  • Independent Saved Views: Each tab retains its own camera selection, grid layout, and arrangement. Select a tab to load the view without rebuilding it for area inspections or event verification.
  • Deletion and the Default Tab: Before deleting a tab, confirm that its camera combination and layout are no longer needed. At least one default tab remains; the last tab cannot be deleted.
  • Keeping Tabs Usable: Organize tabs by area, routine task, or temporary incident. Avoid long, duplicate, or unused names. Use the navigation offered by the page when many tabs are present; scrolling or collapsing behavior depends on the interface.

Typical Uses

  • Create area views for a lobby, parking area, warehouse, entrance, office, or equipment room.
  • Create task views for routine checks, critical areas, night patrol, or alarm verification. Remove temporary event views after the work is complete.
  • Organize views for each responsibility, such as public areas for security, equipment areas for facilities, and an operational overview for management.

Usage Guidelines

  1. Create a tab and give it a clear name.

Contact the site lead, security supervisor, or designated external responder, and communicate verified facts, current risks, actions taken, and unresolved questions.

Response Records

Record at least the verification outcome, conditions at the scene, action taken, operator, and completion time. For false alarms, document the observable cause and use it to review detection regions, thresholds, directions, and schedules. Clearly identify information that remains unverified.

When to Use This Workflow

Export recordings for investigations, alarm reviews, project reporting, or issue escalation. Before exporting, confirm playback and download access for the camera and establish the purpose, recipient, and applicable project privacy and access requirements.

Export Procedure

  1. Locate the event: open Playback using the alarm time, camera, and location. Confirm that the required footage exists and plays.
  2. Review the sequence: inspect the context before, during, and after the event.
  3. Set the trim interval: choose start and end points that preserve the relevant sequence without unnecessary footage.
  4. Export: verify browser download permission, local disk space, and the destination before submitting the export.
  5. Check the file: after the download completes, open it and verify the camera image, timestamp, duration, and full playback.
  6. Register and store: record the event ID, camera, footage interval, exporter, export time, and storage location, and apply the project’s access rules.

File Naming

A useful structure is Date_Time_Location_Camera_EventType_EventID, for example 20260720_143000_Office1F_CAM0460_RestrictedArea_EVT001. This is an example; use the project’s established standard where available. Record the footage interval and time zone so the file can be matched to the source event.

Access and Sharing

  • Export only the cameras and time intervals needed for the task.
  • Avoid unapproved personal cloud drives, messaging tools, or removable devices for transfers.
  • Restrict recipients and storage locations according to project requirements when footage identifies people, work areas, or sensitive facilities.
  • Before external sharing, confirm requirements for approval, redaction, watermarking, or encrypted transfer. These are handling requirements and do not imply that every processing tool is built into the platform.

Troubleshoot an Export

Verify that the source recording exists and plays, the trim interval is valid, downloads are permitted, and the local disk has space. For larger exports, shorten the interval or split it into clips. Check task and download states before submitting another request.

Supported formats, maximum duration, and file-size limits depend on the deployment. If a file will not open, confirm that the download completed, try the project-recommended player, and retain the error, file size, and export interval for investigation.

Least-Privilege Access

Grant only the features and cameras needed for each responsibility. Limit administrator access, user management, system settings, log export, and broad recording downloads to designated roles, and periodically review whether the access is still needed.

Accounts and Shared Workstations

  1. Use individually attributable accounts and avoid prolonged sharing of a privileged account.
  2. Do not save passwords on public, temporary, or shared devices.
  3. Lock the workstation when stepping away and sign out when finished.
  4. Change roles and camera scope, or deactivate access, when responsibilities change, staff leave, or collaboration ends.

Recording, Alarm, and Log Retention

Set retention using camera count, bitrate, capacity, investigation needs, and project policy. Recordings, alarm records, response notes, and logs may have different periods. Preserve the material required for critical events until investigations and audits are complete.

Before shortening a period or capacity limit, check the impact on open investigations. Preserve the required records through the project process, then apply and verify the change. Do not assume that every data type has the same retention window.

Exports and External Sharing

Before exporting recordings, logs, or configuration, confirm the purpose, minimum required scope, recipient, and approval requirements. Accounts and Permissions

Why are some pages or cameras missing after sign-in?

Check both feature permissions and camera-resource permissions for the assigned role. Access to a page does not necessarily include the required cameras. Ask the administrator to verify the access needed for your role.

What if the password-reset email does not arrive?

Use the email address bound to the account, check the spam folder, and use the latest code or link. If the email still does not arrive, ask the administrator to verify the address and mail service.

Cameras and Live Video

Why is there no image when a camera is online?

Online status describes basic connectivity. Check the stream URL, codec, browser media support, and account permissions, then test actual playback.

Why can video quality decrease in a multi-camera view?

Multi-camera views may use a lower-bitrate substream to reduce bandwidth and client load. Switch to a single view or an available higher-quality stream for detail. Supported modes depend on the device and deployment.

Recordings and Exports

Why is no recording available in Playback?

Verify the camera, date, time, and time zone, then check schedule coverage, storage, and recording-task execution. A camera being online now does not establish that footage exists for the interval.

What if an exported video will not open?

Confirm that the download completed and the file size is plausible, then try the project-recommended player. If it still fails, export a shorter interval and retain the error, footage interval, and camera ID.

AI Alarms and Notifications

Why is no alarm triggered even though live video works?

Check algorithm enablement, rule deployment, region, direction, threshold, duration, and active schedule. Validate with an approved, safe example that meets the rule.

Why is a known alarm missing from Notifications?

Check time and status filters, auto refresh, account visibility, and whether the event was closed or archived. Reading a notification or stopping an alert is separate from completing the response.

How should I reduce frequent false alarms?

Retain representative clips and document the causes. Inspect region size, reflections, background motion, occlusion, thresholds, and active periods. Adjust one relevant condition at a time and retest.

Get Help

Start with Troubleshooting. If the issue persists, provide the administrator with the page URL, time and time zone, camera ID, steps, expected and actual results, error text, screenshots, and relevant logs. Remove passwords, tokens, and complete credentials before sharing.
