The platform provides the foundation for an Agentic Vision Intelligence System across security, retail, logistics, and smart-city operations.

AI-Native Video Management

Security and operations teams need to understand what is happening, where it is happening, and which events require a response. POLYTRON ONE connects video management with visual intelligence so teams can move from live footage to detection, investigation, and documented action within the same platform.

Designed as a next-generation VMS, POLYTRON ONE combines real-time analytics, spatial understanding, and intelligent automation. Object detection, behavior analysis, anomaly recognition, and multi-device coordination support the transition from traditional surveillance to autonomous visual intelligence. Camera management, recording review, and access controls provide the operational foundation for those capabilities.

Visual Intelligence Architecture

Multi-Scale Attention Fusion Network (MAFN)

MAFN unifies spatial and semantic cues to support high-precision, context-aware visual analytics. Spatial context concerns where activity occurs and how it relates to the monitored scene. Semantic context concerns the objects, behaviors, and events represented in the video. Combining these cues supports interpretation of activity in relation to its surroundings.

APEX Engine™

The patented APEX Engine™ provides the attention-based AI architecture at the core of POLYTRON ONE. Its role is to support perception, understanding, and action across dynamic environments. MAFN’s fusion of spatial and semantic cues and the APEX Engine™ form the technical foundation for the platform’s context-aware visual intelligence.

In operational use, visual analysis is connected to camera identity, location, detection rules, and event records. Teams can use that context to locate the relevant scene, examine a detection, and determine the appropriate response. Available analytics and integrations depend on the deployed configuration.

From Perception to Response

POLYTRON ONE provides the foundation for an Agentic Vision Intelligence System: autonomous agents that interpret visual conditions, make decisions, and respond in real time across connected operational environments. This approach links perception and contextual understanding to action.

The workflows in this guide make that connection concrete. Configured analytics identify events and trigger alarms; linked video, notifications, and event details provide the context needed for investigation. Operators verify the findings, take the required action, and retain the outcome. The deployment determines which responses are automated and which require operator involvement.

  1. Connect and organize video. Add supported cameras or video sources and maintain their names, groups, and locations so the relevant feeds can be found across modules.
  2. Define the monitoring conditions. Select deployed analytics and configure the applicable regions, directions, thresholds, durations, and active schedules.
  3. Detect and interpret activity. Use object, behavior, and anomaly analysis with spatial and semantic context to identify conditions that need attention.
  4. Initiate and verify the response. Use the alarm, notification, live view, and associated footage to assess the event and carry out the configured response workflow.
  5. Retain the findings. Save classifications, status updates, and handling notes. Review or export the relevant recording when evidence is required.

Platform Capabilities

  • Centralized Video Management: Maintain camera names, connection status, locations, protocols, stream URLs, recording schedules, and alarm settings. Group cameras by building, floor, or operational area so users can find the devices they need for monitoring, playback, and investigation.
  • Live Monitoring and Multiple Views: Arrange feeds in single-camera views, grids, tabs, multiple views, or the real-time 3D view. Switch camera groups for the task, and use location and alarm information to identify the scene that needs attention.
  • AI Video Analysis: Review detections for crowding, line crossing, loitering, falls, unusual behavior, and unattended objects. Documented capabilities also include people counting, face recognition, mask detection, and heat maps. Availability depends on the deployment. Verify relevant detections against the footage.
  • Detection Areas and Rules: Draw detection areas, crossing lines, or restricted zones. Configure supported directions, trigger conditions, durations, people-count thresholds, schedules, and algorithms. For example, monitor entry outside working hours or trigger an alarm when a dwell-time or crowd-size condition is met.
  • Alarm Response: When a rule triggers, the platform links the alarm to live video, notifications, event details, and footage around the event. Operators can confirm, annotate, close, or mark an alarm as false, then follow the applicable work-order or internal response procedure. Retain the handling record for follow-up.
  • Recording Review and Evidence: Find recordings by camera, date, type, and time range. Use playback controls and speed settings to examine the sequence. Review alarm-linked clips for context, and export the relevant interval when evidence is required.
  • Notifications and Event Tracking: Use Notification Center to review severity, time, location, camera, status, and response progress. Filter by keyword, time, status, or type. Open a record to examine footage, AI annotations, and response notes.
  • Accounts, Permissions, and Configuration: Assign roles, feature permissions, and camera access according to each person’s responsibilities. Maintain display preferences, storage, log retention, recording schedules, alarm rules, and system settings. Review access when responsibilities change.
  • System Integration: Connect video sources through protocols such as RTSP and ONVIF. Project configuration determines integration with VMS, 3D visualization, alarm, and business systems, including which video, event, device-status, and detection data can be exchanged.

Login

Sign in with the account assigned by your supplier or administrator. POLYTRON ONE uses the account’s role to determine which pages, cameras, and actions you can access.

Overview

Obtain your assigned username and initial password before first use. Self-registration is not required. Your permissions determine access to Live View, Playback, Cameras, Notification Center, and settings.

First Sign-In

  1. Obtain an account from the supplier, project administrator, or system owner.
  2. Enter the username and initial password on the login page.
  3. If prompted, replace the initial password.
  4. Check that you can open the modules and cameras needed for your work. Contact an administrator if access is missing or incorrect.

Signing In

Enter your username and password on the login page. After authentication, the platform displays the menus, cameras, notifications, and actions available to your role.

Permissions also apply to individual actions. A user without camera-management permission cannot add or edit cameras. A user with view-only access cannot change alarm status or submit handling notes. Access to accounts, roles, logs, and system configuration is controlled separately.

Accounts and Permissions

  1. An authorized administrator configures the required feature permissions and camera access in User Role Settings.
  2. In Account Settings, the administrator creates the account, enters its email address, initial password, and other required details, and assigns a role.
  3. After the administrator saves the account, sign in and verify the required pages, cameras, and actions.

Remember Me

Remember Me can fill in your username and password when you next open the login page on the same trusted device. Use it only on a personal computer or workstation under your control.

Do not save credentials on public computers, shared terminals, or temporary troubleshooting devices. Sign out when you finish.

Password Recovery

Select Forgot Password and verify your identity through the email address linked to the account. Set a new password, then use it to sign in. Resetting the password leaves the account’s role, feature permissions, and camera access unchanged.

Usage Guidelines

  • Sign-in failures: Check the account identifier, username or email address, password, and account and role status. If sign-in succeeds but resources are missing, check the assigned role and camera permissions.
  • Shared workstations: Sign out before leaving, ending a shift, or switching accounts to prevent the next user from continuing your session.
  • Account changes: Replace initial or weak passwords. Change credentials if exposure is suspected, and update roles and camera access when responsibilities or project access change.
  • Reporting issues: Provide the administrator or support team with the account identifier, time of the failure, error message, and a screenshot. Do not include the password.

Forgot Password

Reset a forgotten password through the email address linked to your account. The reset restores sign-in access and preserves your existing role and camera permissions.

Overview

Select Forgot Password on the login page and enter the email address linked to your account. The platform validates the address and sends a verification code or reset link. Complete verification, set a password that meets the displayed requirements, and sign in again.

Reset Your Password

  1. Select Forgot Password on the login page.
  2. Enter the email address linked to your account.
  3. Open the verification email. Use the code or reset link before it expires.
  4. Enter and confirm a new password that meets the deployment’s displayed length and character requirements.
  5. Return to the login page and sign in with the new password. Verify that your existing modules and cameras are available.

Verification and Password Rules

The email address must be linked to a valid account. Codes and reset links expire. Start a new recovery attempt if verification has expired.

After the reset, the old password cannot be used for a new sign-in. Whether sessions on other devices require authentication again depends on the deployed security policy. The reset does not change role, feature, or camera permissions.

Troubleshooting and Precautions

  • No email: Check the address, account association, spam folder, and mail-filtering rules. If the message does not arrive, ask an administrator to check the account email, mail service, and sending configuration.
  • Invalid verification: Use the code or link from the current recovery attempt. Request a new one if it has expired.
  • Password choice: Follow the platform’s password rules. Avoid predictable information such as sequential digits, birthdays, phone numbers, or the account name.
  • Access still fails: Give an administrator the account email, time of the failure, and error message. Do not share passwords or active verification codes.

System Information

Use CPU, memory, and disk indicators to investigate live-video stutter, failed recording writes, slow playback, or delayed page responses. Compare resource readings with the affected cameras, tasks, and logs.

Overview

Live video, AI analysis, recording writes, playback, and notifications use the same system resources. Assess sustained trends together with observed symptoms; a single reading does not establish the cause of a problem.

Resource Indicators

  • CPU Usage: Shows current processor load. If usage stays high, review video decoding, AI analysis, concurrent access, recording tasks, and background services.
  • Memory Usage (RAM): Shows memory and cache pressure. Investigate persistent growth, unusual allocation, or cache that is not released by reviewing service behavior over time.
  • Disk Usage: Shows space consumed by recordings, logs, exports, and cache. Limited free space can affect recording writes, exports, and logging. Review retention and capacity before storage is exhausted.
  • Operational Assessment: Compare resource use with camera counts, AI tasks, recording schedules, and system logs. Use the indicators to guide investigation, then verify the cause in the affected workflow.

When to Use It

  • Review resources during routine checks. For live-video stutter, start with CPU and memory. For slow playback, also consider storage reads.
  • When recordings are missing or exports fail, check free storage. Observe resource changes after adding cameras, AI rules, or recording tasks.
  • If resource pressure persists, give operations staff the relevant trends and logs to assess configuration changes, server upgrades, or additional storage.

Usage Guidelines

Correlate unusual readings with camera availability, AI tasks, recording schedules, alarm records, and system logs. High resource use alone is not a reason to delete footage or interrupt operations.

For sustained CPU or memory pressure, reduce unnecessary concurrent viewing and review task configuration. For rising disk use, inspect recording and log retention, exports, and cache. Clean up or expand storage according to project requirements.

Alarm Overview

Compare total, pending, and resolved alarms to assess outstanding work. Use the notification list to investigate individual events and record their outcomes.

Overview

Alarm counts show event volume and response progress. Assess an individual event using its severity, location, type, and footage. Counts alone do not establish risk.

Indicators and Interpretation

  • Total Alarms: Review the number of recorded alarms in the current reporting scope, including handled and unhandled events, and watch for unusual growth.
  • Pending Alarms: Review events that still need classification, confirmation, or resolution. This count indicates outstanding work, not the severity of each event.
  • Resolved Alarms: Review events that have completed the handling process and compare them with pending work to assess progress.
  • Status and Prioritization: Changing counts can indicate an event surge, response workload, or a need to review classification or detection rules. Prioritize high-severity alarms, personnel safety, critical areas, and recurring event types.
  • Details and Response Review: Use the notification list to inspect type, time, location, camera, state, and related video. For a persistent backlog, review response procedures, staffing, and false-positive patterns.

Review and Handle Alarms

  1. Open notifications and find events by time, status, type, camera, or location.
  2. Open an event’s details. Review the event information, AI annotations, handling notes, and footage before and after the trigger.
  3. Use the available actions to confirm, classify, annotate, close, or mark the event as a False Alarm. Verify that the list and statistics reflect the saved outcome.
  4. After reviewing the records, use the available read or handling action to clear the relevant reminder. Clearing a reminder does not complete an outstanding response.

Usage Guidelines

During a shift, watch for a growing backlog and prioritize high-risk or ongoing incidents. For repeated alarms, review the camera’s rules, detection areas, algorithm settings, and scene conditions.

Use Alarm Overview to identify exceptions and prioritize work. Verify each event and record the response in its notification or alarm details.

Camera Information

Check total, online, and offline camera counts to verify device onboarding, identify outages, and scope an investigation.

Overview

An offline camera may be unable to provide live video, record footage, or run detection. Use changes in the counts to identify the scope of an outage, then inspect the affected devices. Current connectivity does not establish whether footage exists for an earlier period.

Camera Indicators

  • Total Cameras: Compare the number of cameras added to the platform with the site inventory to identify missing, duplicate, or retired devices.
  • Online Cameras: Check how many devices are shown as online. Treat connectivity as a starting point and verify the stream, recording, and analysis capabilities needed for the task.
  • Offline Cameras: Identify devices with interrupted connectivity or unavailable video. Possible causes include power or network issues, invalid stream addresses, credentials or permissions, and service-connection failures.
  • Operational Impact and Investigation: Camera faults can affect live viewing, recording, AI detection, and alarms. Use Camera Management to investigate the device name, IP address, location, protocol, stream URL, group, and permissions.

When to Use It

  • Check camera availability at the start of a shift. When video, recordings, or expected alarms are missing, check the affected camera’s status first.
  • After deployment, bulk onboarding, or device changes, compare the platform with the site inventory. If several cameras go offline together, review shared network and service dependencies and correlate system logs.

Troubleshooting

  1. In Camera Management, verify the IP address, power, network connection, protocol, stream URL, and credentials. If multiple cameras fail at the same time, also investigate switches, VMS services, and server connections.
  2. Resolve count discrepancies by checking for missing, duplicate, retired, or incorrectly grouped devices. Use discovery or manual maintenance as appropriate.
  3. If an online camera has no image or recording, check stream settings, codecs, protocol compatibility, and browser playback support. Verify the actual feed rather than relying on the online indicator.

View Operations

Inspect live video with snapshots, stream switching, analysis, fullscreen, recording indicators, multiple views, and camera details. Available controls depend on the device and your access.

Overview

Camera type, connection protocol, video status, and account permissions determine which actions are available. Multiple views require a compatible 360° camera; PTZ and 3D controls require the corresponding capabilities and configuration.

Available Actions

  • Real-Time 3D: Open the roaming or 3D scene view to relate the camera feed to buildings, floors, areas, and device locations.
  • Snapshot: Capture the current main video image and save or download it locally to retain a scene for later review.
  • Switch to Substream: A substream generally uses less bandwidth at lower image quality. Use it for constrained networks, multiple simultaneous feeds, or resource pressure.
  • Video Analysis: Inspect detection boxes, target recognition, people counts, and analysis such as line crossing, loitering, or falls to understand a detection or alarm.
  • Fullscreen: Enlarge the current video for scene inspection, event verification, or review.
  • Recording State: Use the recording indicator to check whether recording is active. To verify historical coverage, open Playback and inspect the required period.
  • Multiple Views: POLYTRON 360° cameras can show multiple directional views. Select a subview to make that direction the main view and inspect another part of the same space.
  • Camera Details and State: Review the camera’s name, location, group, state, and associated position, and maintain relevant details in Camera Management. Distinguish playing, loading, stream error, offline, unauthorized, and recording indicators before troubleshooting.

Usage Guidelines

  1. For an incident, inspect the scene in a single pane or fullscreen. Capture a snapshot, review analysis annotations, or open the associated 3D location when the task requires it.
  2. If feeds stutter or bandwidth is constrained, switch to a substream and check whether playback improves. Return to an appropriate stream quality when you need to inspect detail.
  3. For a 360° camera, use multiple views to inspect the relevant directions. If a control is unavailable, verify the camera’s capabilities, protocol, recording configuration, and permissions before reporting a fault.

01. Geo Fencing

Use Geo Fencing to review people or other supported targets entering or remaining in a restricted area. The fence is a region drawn in the video image; it does not represent a GPS boundary. A target must meet the deployed region rule during its active schedule.

01 / Observe

Outside the Region

A target approaches the configured boundary.

02 / Evaluate

Boundary Interaction

The target enters the selected region.

03 / Review

Qualifying Presence

The configured region rule is satisfied.

Set Up and Check: Draw the region around the actual restricted space. Exclude public walkways, reflections, and areas beyond the camera’s useful view. Configure direction and duration only where the selected rule provides those controls.

Interpret the Result: Check the target’s position before and after the boundary interaction. A target already visible inside the region does not, by itself, establish when entry occurred.

02. People Counting

Use People Counting to assess the number of people visible within a defined region. The same person moving within the region should remain one observed occupant. Area occupancy and cumulative entry or exit totals answer different questions; use the interpretation supported by the deployed counting rule.

01 / Observe

Current Occupants

Two people are visible inside the region.

02 / Evaluate

New Arrival

A third person enters the monitored region.

03 / Review

Current Count

Movement within the region does not add a person.

Set Up and Check: Select a region where people remain distinguishable and check the camera angle, occlusion, and lighting. Set a people-count threshold when the selected rule exposes that setting. Do not treat an illustrative count in the diagram as a recommended limit.

Interpret the Result: Compare the observed count with the scene as people enter, remain, and leave. Passage totals, where supported, do not identify unique individuals.

03. No-Mask Detection

Use No-Mask Detection in a defined area and monitoring period where people are required to wear masks. The rule evaluates whether a sufficiently visible face shows the required covering over the nose and mouth. Repeated usable observations help distinguish a persistent omission from a brief unusable view.

01 / Observe

Covered Face

The visible nose and mouth are covered.

02 / Evaluate

Visible Omission

An unobstructed face lacks the required mask.

03 / Review

Review the Condition

Usable observations retain the omission for review.

Set Up and Check: Choose a camera position that provides a useful view of faces. Apply the region and active schedule to the relevant work area. Configure any available confirmation interval according to the deployed rule.

Interpret the Result: Head rotation, hands, and other obstructions can prevent a reliable observation. A visible mask does not establish its filtration performance or effectiveness.

04. Smoking Detection

Smoking Detection evaluates visible smoking-related objects together with hand-to-mouth interaction over time. A repeated interaction involving a supported smoking-device candidate provides more useful evidence than a hand near the face in a single frame. Treat the result as suspected smoking that requires review.

01 / Observe

Object Candidate

A narrow object is visible near the hand.

02 / Evaluate

Hand-to-Mouth Interaction

The object moves toward the mouth.

03 / Review

Suspected Episode

Repeated contextual cues support operator review.

Set Up and Check: Cover the relevant no-smoking area and confirm that the camera can resolve the object and the person’s hand and mouth. Use the supported schedule and trigger-duration controls where available.

Interpret the Result: Eating, drinking, and background smoke can resemble parts of the sequence. Do not assume that every tobacco product or electronic device is supported; check the deployed detection and review the associated footage.

05. Motion Detection

Use Motion Detection when the monitoring task concerns movement in a region rather than a particular behavior. The rule evaluates visible changes over time within the configured area. A motion observation alone does not establish that a person entered a restricted area, crossed a line, or performed a specific action.

01 / Observe

Reference View

Observe the monitored part of the scene.

02 / Evaluate

Visible Change

A moving subject changes the image.

03 / Review

Review the Activity

Check the source of the visible movement.

Set Up and Check: Restrict the region to the relevant scene and apply the required schedule. Use duration and other controls only when they are provided by the selected rule. Test both expected activity and normal background movement.

Interpret the Result: Lighting changes, shadows, reflections, moving foliage, and camera movement can create visible change. Review the footage and use a more specific supported detection when the task requires a semantic event.

06. Line Crossing

Line Crossing evaluates a tracked target moving from one side of a finite virtual line to the other. The observed trajectory must cross the configured line segment. Being close to the line or first appearing on one side does not establish a crossing.

01 / Observe

Starting Side

A target is observed on one side of the line.

02 / Evaluate

Crossing Path

Its trajectory intersects the finite line.

03 / Review

Opposite Side

The continued track confirms the crossing.

Set Up and Check: Place the line across the relevant passage and ensure that both sides remain visible. Select Entry, Exit, or Both Directions where the rule provides those options, and verify the direction against actual movement at the site.

Interpret the Result: Check the sequence before and after the crossing. Occlusion, a line extending beyond the usable scene, or repeated back-and-forth movement can affect interpretation; validate how the deployed rule handles repeated crossings.

07. Facial Detection

Facial Detection identifies a usable facial region in the camera image. Face size, visibility, lighting, and image quality affect whether the region can be evaluated. The result establishes face presence; it does not, by itself, identify a person or verify access authorization.

01 / Observe

Face Enters View

A person moves into the usable camera view.

02 / Evaluate

Locate the Facial Region

Visible detail supports face localization.

03 / Review

Confirm Face Presence

Review the observed facial region.

Set Up and Check: Choose an angle and distance that preserve useful facial detail. Keep the relevant area visible and use only the face-specific controls exposed by the deployed detection.

Interpret the Result: Inspect the localized facial region in its scene context. A profile view, face covering, backlighting, or occlusion can limit the observation. Keep face detection distinct from face recognition or identity matching.

08. IDLE Time

IDLE Time concerns a person’s dwell time in a defined region. A maintained track provides evidence that the same observed person remains in the area over the monitoring interval. Movement within the region can still count as continued presence; this label does not describe equipment idle time.

01 / Observe

Presence Begins

A person enters the timed region.

02 / Evaluate

Presence Continues

The maintained track remains inside.

03 / Review

Dwell Allowance Exceeded

Continued presence exceeds the configured allowance.

Set Up and Check: Draw the region around the relevant waiting or restricted space and configure the dwell interval where the deployed rule supports it. Distinguish a dwell allowance from the separate VA Trigger Duration setting; do not assume they are interchangeable.

Interpret the Result: Check the start of presence, the continued track, and any exits or occlusion gaps. Confirm how the deployed rule treats reentry and interrupted observations before interpreting an excessive-dwell event.

09. Fire Detection

Fire Detection evaluates visible flame appearance and its development over time. A persistent flame-like region provides more context than a single bright area. The observation concerns visible fire evidence in the image; it does not establish combustion chemistry or detect a hidden fire outside the camera view.

01 / Observe

Flame-Like Appearance

A small flame-like region becomes visible.

02 / Evaluate

Persistence and Development

The visible region continues over time.

03 / Review

Review Visible Fire Evidence

The qualifying observation is reviewed in context.

Set Up and Check: Cover the relevant risk area and examine any legitimate flame sources within it. Apply regions, schedules, exclusions, and confirmation controls only to the extent supported by the deployed rule.

Interpret the Result: Review the surrounding sequence for persistent flame characteristics and scene context. Reflections, bright lighting, welding, or permitted flames may require closer inspection. Fire Detection does not by itself establish that a separate smoke-detection capability is active.

10. Crowd Detection

Crowd Detection can identify a crowding condition when the observed number of people meets the deployed threshold rule for the required interval. Use it to review continued occupancy beyond the intended limit. A single busy frame does not establish persistent overcrowding, and crowding is different from a group forming.

01 / Observe

Below the Limit

The example starts with three visible people.

02 / Evaluate

Count Increases

New arrivals raise the observed count.

03 / Review

Crowding Persists

The configured count condition persists over time.

Set Up and Check: Define the monitored region, people-count threshold, and duration where those controls are supported. Use a count threshold as a number of people; do not describe it as people per square meter without an established spatial calibration.

Interpret the Result: Check visibility, occlusion, arrivals, and departures over the relevant interval. Distinguish a temporary passage through the scene from a sustained crowding condition.

11. Fall Detection

Fall Detection evaluates a sequence in which a person descends and changes posture relative to the floor or another supporting surface. The preceding posture and the observed transition help distinguish a possible fall from a person simply being low in the image. A fall need not be rapid or followed by prolonged immobility.

01 / Observe

Initial Posture

The person and supporting floor are visible.

02 / Evaluate

Observed Descent

Body height and orientation change.

03 / Review

Review a Possible Fall

The descent and low posture are reviewed together.

Set Up and Check: Use a view that shows the person and the supporting surface. Apply the relevant region and schedule only when this detection is available in the deployed library. Validate with suitable recorded examples rather than staging a dangerous movement.

Interpret the Result: Check the preceding frames and the subsequent scene. Sitting, kneeling, or picking up an object can resemble parts of the sequence. A person first observed on the floor does not establish that the preceding fall was seen.

12. Sleeping Detection

Sleeping Detection combines persistent visual cues such as a lowered head, slumped posture, and reduced activity during an active duty period. A sustained combination of cues can support a possible-sleep event for review. Inactivity alone does not establish that a person is asleep.

01 / Observe

Active Attendance

An upright worker is visible at the workstation.

02 / Evaluate

Posture Changes

The head lowers and the seated posture slumps.

03 / Review

Review Persistent Cues

Posture and reduced activity persist together.

Set Up and Check: Use a camera angle that shows the relevant workstation and posture. Limit the active schedule to periods requiring attendance and account for authorized rest. Configure any confirmation duration only if the deployed rule provides it.

Interpret the Result: Compare the posture and activity before and during the observation. Reading, looking down, or a briefly lowered head may resemble sleep indicators. The event is a visual observation for human review, not a clinical determination of sleep.

Use Cases

  • Restricted areas: monitor entry or boundary crossing around equipment rooms, warehouses, construction zones, and controlled spaces.
  • Crowding: apply appropriate people-count thresholds and crowd rules in lobbies, dining areas, exhibition spaces, or public areas.
  • Entrances: detect entry, exit, or both, and assess exceptions against the authorization rules used in the project.
  • Behavior monitoring: use deployed capabilities for loitering, falls, unattended objects, or other specified behaviors.
  • After-hours monitoring: activate selected rules during nights, rest days, or other defined periods.

Procedure and Validation

  1. Identify the camera, target behavior, and active period. Verify that the live preview shows the intended location.
  2. Select the algorithm. Draw the region or line, then configure direction, threshold, duration, and schedule as applicable.
  3. Deploy the rule. Where permitted and safe, observe examples that should trigger it and examples that should not.
  4. Check the live indicator, notification, and event details. Verify that the source, timestamp, and detected behavior match the test.
  5. For excessive or missing alarms, inspect the region, duration, people-count threshold, direction, schedule, and algorithm. Check lighting, occlusion, reflections, and passing traffic. Change one relevant setting at a time, then review the result.

When to Use This Workflow

Export footage for investigations, alarm reviews, project reporting, or issue escalation. Before exporting, verify playback and download access for the camera. Establish the purpose, recipient, and applicable project privacy and access requirements.

Export Procedure

  1. Locate the event: Open Playback using the alarm time, camera, and location. Verify that the required footage exists and plays.
  2. Review the sequence: Watch the context before, during, and after the event.
  3. Set the trim interval: Choose start and end points that retain the relevant sequence without unnecessary footage.
  4. Export: Check browser download permission, free disk space, and the destination before submitting the export.
  5. Check the file: After the download completes, open the file. Verify the camera image, timestamp, duration, and playback through the full clip.
  6. Register and store: Record the event ID, camera, footage interval, exporter, export time, and storage location. Apply the project’s access rules.

  • Export only the cameras and intervals needed for the task.

  • Before external sharing, verify approval, redaction, watermarking, or encrypted-transfer requirements. These requirements do not imply that every processing tool is built into the platform.

Troubleshoot an Export

Check that the source footage exists and plays, the trim interval is valid, downloads are permitted, and the local disk has space. For larger exports, shorten the interval or split it into clips. Inspect task and download status before submitting another request.
